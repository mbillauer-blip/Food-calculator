**The short answer:** budget 3–5 ribs per adult depending on how many other dishes are being served, then round up to full racks — butchers and grocery stores don't sell partial racks.

## Why "ribs per person" isn't the number you actually need

Ribs are sold and cooked by the rack, not individually. Calculating "3.5 ribs per person" is correct math but useless at the checkout counter — you need to know how many *racks* to buy. That means two steps: figure out total ribs needed, then divide by ribs-per-rack and round up.

## Step 1: Estimate ribs per adult

Like any protein, the right portion depends on how much else is on the table:

- **Ribs are the main event (few other dishes):** ~5 ribs per adult
- **A few sides and carbs alongside:** ~4 ribs per adult
- **Buffet-style spread with lots of other food:** ~3 ribs per adult

Kids typically eat about 55% of an adult portion — so roughly 2–3 ribs per child guest, following the same scaling.

## Step 2: Adjust for lamb specifically

Lamb is a premium cut that guests tend to prioritize over other proteins on the table, so it's worth padding the estimate by 15–20% compared to a more everyday rib (like pork). If lamb ribs are served as a passed appetizer rather than a main course, guests will also eat more of them simply because appetizers get picked at for longer over the course of the night — another 20–25% on top is a reasonable adjustment.

## Step 3: Convert total ribs to racks

A standard rack of lamb has **6 ribs**. Once you know your total rib count across all guests:

```
racks needed = ceil(total ribs ÷ 6)
```

Always round up — a partial rack isn't purchasable, and running short on the main course is worse than having one extra rack in the fridge.

## Worked example

10 adults and 4 kids, lamb ribs served as an appetizer alongside one other appetizer:

1. Base rate: ~4 ribs/adult (two appetizer varieties)
2. Lamb premium adjustment: ×1.2 → 4.8 ribs/adult
3. Appetizer late-night adjustment: ×1.25 → 6 ribs/adult
4. Total ribs: (6 × 10 adults) + (6 × 0.55 × 4 kids) = 73.2 ribs
5. Racks needed: ceil(73.2 ÷ 6) = **13 racks**
